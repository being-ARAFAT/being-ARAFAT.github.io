About

A student learning graphic design the hard way — by making things, breaking them, and making them again.

I got into design almost by accident — building posters for a school event because nobody else wanted to, and realizing halfway through that I actually liked figuring out why one layout worked and another didn't. Everything since has been self-taught: Canva first, then Photoshop and Illustrator, learned through tutorials, trial and error, and a lot of projects nobody paid me for.

Most of what I know comes from real situations — directing a school festival's entire visual identity in a few weeks, or a club needing a poster by Thursday. That pressure taught me more about design decisions than any course could. Volunteering with Red Crescent Youth keeps the work grounded in something more than aesthetics.

I'm still early in this. But I've shipped real work for a real school community, led a creative team, and I take the outcome seriously even when the deadline is tight.

Case study: TGHS Trivium Festival

How the school's flagship event got a visual identity that held together end to end.

One mark, applied everywhere it needed to show up.

The TGHS Trivium Festival needed an identity that could hold together across a stage banner, a t-shirt print, and an Instagram grid — without looking like three different events. This is the project I'd point to first.

Facebook@TGHSACC
01

Brief

As Director, I needed a complete identity — logo, banners, apparel, and social assets — in time for the festival's launch.

02

Idea

Built the concept around momentum and celebration — a mark that could scale from a small badge to a stage-sized banner.

03

Process

Sketched several directions first, then narrowed to digital roughs and tested them against real signage sizes.

04

Design development

Refined the mark's geometry, locked a small color system, and paired it with a bold display typeface for legibility at distance.

05

Final result

A flexible mark and a small type and color system, with usage rules simple enough for a student team to apply consistently.

06

Applications

Carried across entrance banners, event t-shirts, standees, and a full week of social posts.
